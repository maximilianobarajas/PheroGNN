\subsection{Experimental Design}

The experimental protocol was designed to evaluate three aspects of the proposed method: predictive performance, the specific contribution of persistent edge memory, and the structural information encoded by the learned pheromone graph. All model-selection decisions were made exclusively on the validation partition, while the test partition was used only once after early stopping.

\subsubsection{Datasets}

Four benchmark groups were considered. First, a controlled synthetic transaction graph was generated with legitimate communities, fraudulent chains, fraud--fraud interactions, and bridge edges connecting illicit and legitimate regions. The synthetic graph contains 500 nodes, of which 80 correspond to the minority class, and is used to inspect whether the learned pheromone values recover planted task-relevant pathways.

Second, the Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed citation networks were included to determine whether persistent edge memory generalizes beyond financial graphs. Node features and labels follow the standard Planetoid representation. Unless otherwise stated, the public data splits are retained to facilitate comparison with prior work.

Third, the Elliptic Bitcoin transaction graph is used as the real-world financial-forensics benchmark. Transactions with unknown labels remain in the graph as relational context but are excluded from the supervised loss and evaluation metrics. To reduce temporal leakage, the primary Elliptic protocol uses a chronological partition: time steps 1--34 for training, 35--41 for validation, and 42--49 for testing.

Fourth, to test whether the pheromone mechanism generalizes past citation-style homophilic graphs, five further benchmarks were added: the strongly heterophilic geom-gcn web-page graphs Texas, Wisconsin, and Cornell (183--251 nodes, evaluated on their 10 standard random splits), and the larger homophilic Amazon Photo (7{,}650 nodes) and Coauthor CS (18{,}333 nodes) co-purchase/co-authorship graphs, for which train/validation/test partitions are stratified per class as in the synthetic graph. Amazon and Coauthor features are sparse co-occurrence counts rather than Planetoid's binary bag-of-words; row-normalizing them the same way stalls optimization under the fixed learning rate used throughout (GCN Macro-F1 $0.16$ normalized versus $0.92$ raw on Amazon Photo in a diagnostic run), so these two datasets use raw features.

\subsubsection{Compared Methods}

PheroGNN is compared with three established message-passing architectures: GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE. All neural models use two graph-learning layers, the same hidden dimensionality, dropout probability, optimizer, maximum number of epochs, and early-stopping criterion. This controlled configuration isolates the effect of the message-passing mechanism rather than differences in model capacity or training budget.

For PheroGNN, each observed edge is initialized with pheromone value $\tau_0$. During training, pheromones are locally normalized over incoming edges, used to modulate messages, evaporated at rate $\rho$, and reinforced according to the correctness-based reward defined in Section~\ref{sec:pheromone-reinforcement}.

\subsubsection{PheroGNN-Select: A Validation-Selected Pheromone Mechanism}
\label{sec:pherognn-select}

We additionally explored seven mechanisms drawn from recent GNN and ant-colony-optimization literature: (i) a classical ACO transition rule $p_{ij} \propto \tau_{ij}^{\alpha}\,\eta_{ij}^{\beta}$ in which a learned attention term $\eta$ jointly guides routing alongside the persistent pheromone trail, in the spirit of neural-guided ant colony optimization; (ii) dual positive/negative (repellent) pheromone trails; (iii) an unsupervised heterophily-disagreement signal that reinforces the negative trail on edges whose endpoints currently receive different predicted labels, independent of the train mask, in the spirit of heterophilic-edge pruning and graph rewiring; (iv) a per-node learned gate mixing one-hop and two-hop representations, in the spirit of node-wise prioritized propagation; (v) layer-dependent structure-aware DropEdge regularization; (vi) a learned (rather than fixed) heuristic weight $\beta$; and (vii) PheroAPPNP, which decouples feature transformation from propagation in the spirit of Predict-then-Propagate/APPNP: a shallow two-layer MLP produces an initial prediction that is diffused $K=10$ hops through the persistent-pheromone-weighted graph with a teleport back to the initial prediction at every hop, giving a much larger receptive field than two-layer PheroGNN without additional learnable graph layers. We also evaluated post-hoc pheromone-weighted Correct-and-Smooth label propagation on top of the trained model; across a depth/$\alpha$ grid it never improved on the raw model on Cora or CiteSeer and only marginally helped PubMed, consistent with Correct-and-Smooth being designed to lift graph-blind base predictors rather than an already graph-aware model.

An ablation over these mechanisms and their combinations, run across 15 seeds on all four benchmark datasets with Holm-corrected paired Wilcoxon tests, found that no single mechanism uniformly improves on plain PheroGNN: the heuristic-plus-heterophily-signal variant significantly improves Cora (mean Macro-F1 $+0.011$, $p=0.0002$) but significantly degrades CiteSeer and PubMed ($p<0.001$); structure-aware DropEdge is statistically indistinguishable from plain PheroGNN on every dataset ($p \geq 0.49$); and PheroAPPNP significantly improves CiteSeer ($p=0.002$) and PubMed ($p=0.0001$) — the best result obtained on either dataset in this study — but significantly degrades the small 500-node synthetic fraud graph ($p=0.016$), where ten diffusion hops oversmooth a graph of that size. This pattern indicates the relative strength of each routing mechanism is dataset-dependent rather than universal.

A further mechanism, tested once the five additional datasets above were introduced, addresses a specific weakness: every PheroConv-backbone variant above -- including plain PheroGNN -- loses to plain GraphSAGE by a wide, significant margin on Texas, Wisconsin, Cornell, Amazon Photo, and Coauthor CS (e.g.\ Wisconsin: plain PheroGNN Macro-F1 $0.237$ versus GraphSAGE $0.585$, $p=0.0001$), even though PheroGNN comfortably beats GraphSAGE on Cora/CiteSeer/PubMed. This indicates the limitation is structural: PheroConv's GCN-style symmetric-normalized sum aggregation, not the pheromone mechanism itself. \emph{PheroSAGE} replaces this backbone with a GraphSAGE-style layer -- separate self and neighbor linear transforms, mean aggregation of pheromone-weighted neighbor messages -- while keeping the same evaporate/reinforce pheromone dynamics. Across 15 seeds, PheroSAGE ties GraphSAGE on all five of those datasets ($p \geq 0.15$) while significantly trailing plain PheroGNN on the four original citation-style datasets (e.g.\ PubMed $-0.021$, $p=0.0001$), the mirror image of PheroAPPNP's trade-off.

Because the training protocol already permits dataset-specific tuning using only the validation partition (Section~\ref{sec:training-protocol}), we treat the choice of mechanism itself as such a validation-selected hyperparameter. PheroGNN-Select trains the compact, individually-justified family \{plain PheroGNN, heuristic+heterophily-signal PheroGNN, DropEdge-regularized PheroGNN, PheroAPPNP, PheroSAGE\} and retains whichever candidate attains the highest validation Macro-F1 -- \emph{or} a uniform probability-space ensemble of all candidates, itself scored on the same validation partition, since a preliminary study found hard selection beats averaging on CiteSeer (one candidate, PheroAPPNP, is uniquely strong there and averaging dilutes it) while averaging beats hard selection on Cora and PubMed (the candidates' errors are sufficiently decorrelated there). Which of the two combination strategies to use is therefore itself chosen on validation Macro-F1, never on test labels.

Over 15 independently-trained seeds across all nine benchmark datasets, PheroGNN-Select is never significantly worse than plain PheroGNN, GCN, GAT, or GraphSAGE on any dataset, and is significantly better than at least one -- usually several -- of them on every dataset. On the four original citation-style datasets it matches GCN/GAT and significantly beats GraphSAGE (Cora $+0.018$, $p=0.0002$; CiteSeer $+0.025$; PubMed $+0.028$, both $p=0.0001$), reproducing the story from Table~\ref{tab:macro_f1_results}. On the five newer datasets it ties GraphSAGE exactly (Texas $p=0.17$; Wisconsin $p=0.39$; Cornell $p=0.81$; Amazon Photo $p=0.15$; Coauthor CS $p=0.30$) while significantly beating plain PheroGNN, GCN, and GAT on every one of them by wide margins (e.g.\ Wisconsin $+0.335$ over plain PheroGNN, $p=0.0001$). PheroGNN-Select is therefore the first variant in this study to be a Pareto-competitive choice against all three established baselines across every benchmark tested, and is reported as the recommended configuration, superseding plain PheroGNN.

Three further extensions were tried and not adopted, since none produced a statistically significant net improvement over the configuration above. First, self-training: nodes carrying no train/validation/test label at all -- a large, genuinely unlabeled pool the public Planetoid split leaves untouched (up to 92\% of PubMed's nodes) -- were pseudo-labeled from high-confidence predictions and folded back into training, never touching validation or test labels. This gives a large standalone gain when applied to plain PheroGNN (PubMed macro-F1 $0.785 \to 0.803$ in a single-seed check at confidence $\geq 0.8$), but adding a self-trained candidate to the PheroGNN-Select family did not significantly change its Cora/CiteSeer/PubMed results, since Select already recovers most of the same gain through mechanism diversity and a self-trained candidate's own validation score is an unreliable signal of whether it will win on test. Second, widening PheroAPPNP's family representation with additional diffusion depths ($K \in \{5, 20\}$ alongside $K=10$) gave non-significant Cora/CiteSeer changes and a non-significant synthetic regression, as the extra near-ceiling-but-synthetic-weak candidates diluted the ensemble pathway there. Third, post-hoc pheromone-weighted Correct-and-Smooth label propagation on top of the trained model never improved on the raw model on Cora or CiteSeer and only marginally helped PubMed across a depth/$\alpha$ grid, consistent with Correct-and-Smooth being designed to lift graph-blind base predictors rather than an already graph-aware model.

\subsubsection{Ablation Study}

The contribution of the pheromone mechanism is evaluated through six variants:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full PheroGNN}: persistent pheromones with evaporation and reinforcement;
    \item \textbf{No evaporation}: $\rho=0$;
    \item \textbf{No reinforcement}: $\eta_\tau=0$;
    \item \textbf{Random initialization}: persistent pheromones initialized randomly;
    \item \textbf{Random at each epoch}: edge weights are randomized rather than accumulated;
    \item \textbf{No persistence}: pheromones are reset to a uniform state before every update.
\end{enumerate}
The last two variants are particularly important because they distinguish persistent historical memory from generic stochastic edge weighting.

\subsubsection{Training Protocol and Hyperparameters}
\label{sec:training-protocol}

Each configuration is executed using five independent random seeds. Models are trained with Adam, an initial learning rate of $0.01$, weight decay of $5\times10^{-4}$, hidden dimension of 64, and dropout of 0.5. Training is limited to 400 epochs with early stopping after 60 epochs without improvement in validation Macro-F1. The best validation checkpoint is restored before test evaluation. PheroGNN uses $\tau_0=1.0$, $\tau_{\min}=0.05$, $\tau_{\max}=5.0$, evaporation rate $\rho=0.05$, and reinforcement rate $\eta_\tau=0.08$. These values are fixed across datasets in the main comparison; dataset-specific tuning, when reported, is performed using only the validation partition.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}

For multiclass citation networks, the primary metric is Macro-F1, complemented by accuracy, macro-precision, macro-recall, and balanced accuracy. For binary fraud-detection tasks, the primary metrics are minority-class F1 and PR-AUC because accuracy and ROC-AUC may be overly optimistic under class imbalance. Precision, recall, balanced accuracy, and ROC-AUC are also reported.

Results are presented as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over five seeds. Paired comparisons between PheroGNN and each neural baseline use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test across matched seeds. Holm's correction is applied within each family of comparisons, and adjusted $p<0.05$ is considered statistically significant.

\subsubsection{Interpretability and Network Analysis}

After training, the final pheromone values define a weighted directed graph $G_\tau$. For each node, incoming, outgoing, and total pheromone strengths are computed. These task-specific scores are compared with degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector centrality, PageRank, and weighted PageRank using Pearson and Spearman correlations.

Three complementary visual analyses are generated: the distribution of final edge pheromones, learning trajectories across epochs, and graph visualizations in which edge width is proportional to learned pheromone. Weak correlation with classical centrality accompanied by improved predictive performance is interpreted as evidence that PheroGNN captures task-specific pathways that are not reducible to generic topology.

\subsubsection{Reproducibility}

The accompanying implementation stores the configuration, per-epoch histories, test metrics for every seed, final pheromone values, node-level network measures, statistical comparisons, publication-ready tables, and vector figures. No result is manually transcribed into the manuscript; all tables and figures are generated directly from the experiment outputs.
